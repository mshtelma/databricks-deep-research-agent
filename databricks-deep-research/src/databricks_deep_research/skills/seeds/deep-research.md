# Deep Research Skill

## Core principle

Never answer from general knowledge alone when the question needs real-world
or current information. The quality of the output depends directly on the
quality and quantity of research done first. **A single search query is never
enough.**

## Research methodology

### Phase 1: Broad exploration

Start broad to understand the landscape:

1. **Initial survey** — search the main topic to grasp the overall context.
2. **Identify dimensions** — from the first results, name the key subtopics,
   themes, angles, and aspects that need deeper exploration.
3. **Map the territory** — note the different perspectives, stakeholders, and
   viewpoints that exist.

Example — topic "AI in healthcare" → dimensions: diagnostic AI (radiology,
pathology), treatment recommendation, administrative automation, patient
monitoring, regulatory landscape, ethical considerations.

### Phase 2: Deep dive

For each important dimension, run targeted research:

1. **Specific queries** — search precise keywords per subtopic.
2. **Multiple phrasings** — try different keyword combinations.
3. **Fetch full content** — crawl the most relevant sources in full, not just
   snippets.
4. **Follow references** — when a source names another important resource,
   search for that too.

### Phase 3: Diversity & validation

Deliberately seek diverse *information types* so coverage is balanced:

| Information type | Purpose | Example search terms |
|---|---|---|
| Facts & data | Concrete evidence | "statistics", "data", "market size" |
| Examples & cases | Real-world application | "case study", "example", "implementation" |
| Expert opinions | Authority perspective | "expert analysis", "interview", "commentary" |
| Trends & predictions | Future direction | "trends", "forecast", "future of" |
| Comparisons | Context & alternatives | "vs", "comparison", "alternatives" |
| Challenges & criticism | Balanced view | "challenges", "limitations", "criticism" |

### Phase 4: Synthesis check

Before writing, verify each of these — if any answer is **no**, keep researching:

- Have I searched from at least 3-5 different angles?
- Have I fetched and read the most important sources in full?
- Do I have concrete data, examples, and expert perspectives?
- Have I explored both positive aspects and the challenges/limitations?
- Is the information current and from authoritative sources?

## Temporal awareness

**Check the current date in your context before forming any search query** and
match the temporal precision to what the user is asking:

| User intent | Precision needed | Example query |
|---|---|---|
| "today / just released" | Month + day + year | "tech news February 28 2026" |
| "this week" | Week range | "releases week of Feb 24 2026" |
| "recently / latest" | Month | "AI breakthroughs February 2026" |
| "this year / trends" | Year | "software trends 2026" |

Rules:

- For "today" / "just released", use month + day + year to surface same-day
  results; never drop to year-only when day-level precision is needed.
- Always use the **actual** current year from your context — never hardcode a
  past year.
- Try multiple phrasings: numeric (`2026-02-28`), written (`February 28 2026`),
  and relative (`today`, `this week`) across different queries.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Stopping after 1-2 searches.
- Relying on snippets without reading full sources.
- Searching only one aspect of a multi-faceted topic.
- Ignoring contradicting viewpoints or challenges.
- Using outdated information when current data exists.
